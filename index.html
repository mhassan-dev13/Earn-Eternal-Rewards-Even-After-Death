Continuous Rewards

Earn Eternal Rewards Even After Death

The Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) said: "When a man dies, his deeds come to an end except for three things: Sadaqah Jariyah (ceaseless charity); a knowledge which is beneficial, or a virtuous descendant who prays for him."

Explore 6 Ways

6 Acts of Continuous Charity

Invest in your hereafter by implementing these easy, highly-rewarding deeds today.

1. Gift a Holy Qur'an

Provide a copy of the Holy Qur'an to an individual, a school, or a local mosque. Every single time a letter from it is recited, an immense spiritual reward is directly credited to your account.

2. Donate a Wheelchair

Donate mobility equipment or medical resources to a hospital or clinic. Every single instance an ailing, sick, or elderly person finds comfort and uses it, you gain persistent blessings.

3. Build a Masjid

Participate financially or physically in building or maintaining a mosque. You earn continuous rewards for every single prayer, prostration, and remembrance of Allah made inside it.

4. Provide Clean Water

Place a functioning water cooler, filtration plant, or dig a water well in a crowded public space. Providing access to clean water is highly emphasized as one of the best forms of charity.

5. Plant a Tree

Plant a tree or a crop field. Every time a human seeker, a passing traveler, a bird, or an animal consumes its fresh fruit or finds comfortable shade underneath it, you gain automated continuous reward.

6. Spread Beneficial Knowledge

And the easiest method: Share this message with others. Simply spreading beneficial knowledge grants you equivalent rewards whenever an inspired individual acts upon any item listed here.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers about Sadaqah Jariyah and good deeds — with references.

Sadaqah Jariyah means ongoing charity — a good deed whose reward keeps flowing for as long as people benefit from it, even after the giver has passed away. It is different from a one-time donation: a well keeps giving water, a masjid keeps hosting prayers, and knowledge keeps being passed on. Reference: Sahih Muslim, Hadith 1631 — the Prophet (ﷺ) said that when a person dies, their deeds end except for three things: ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, or a righteous child who prays for them.

Yes. You can give charity on behalf of a deceased parent or loved one, and the reward reaches them. One well-known example is giving water: when Sa'd ibn 'Ubadah asked the Prophet (ﷺ) about the best charity for his late mother, he was told that giving water is among the best. Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 1388 (charity on behalf of a deceased parent); Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1679 (giving water as charity).

The best act is the one you can do sincerely and consistently. A small deed done regularly is more beloved to Allah than a large deed done once and abandoned. So choose an act you can repeat — even a small monthly donation, a tree planted every year, or knowledge shared every week. Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 6465 — "The most beloved deeds to Allah are the most consistent, even if small."

Yes. Every prayer, recitation, and remembrance of Allah that takes place in a masjid you helped build or maintain becomes part of your ongoing reward. You do not need to fund an entire masjid — even a small contribution towards its construction or upkeep counts. Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 450; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 533 — "Whoever builds a mosque for Allah, Allah will build for him a house in Paradise."

Absolutely. Sadaqah Jariyah is not only about money. Planting a tree costs almost nothing, yet as long as any person, bird, or animal benefits from its shade or fruit, your reward continues. Likewise, sharing beneficial knowledge — teaching someone a skill, a verse, or a good habit — costs nothing but keeps rewarding you. Reference: Sahih Muslim, Hadith 1552 — no Muslim plants a tree from which a bird, person, or animal eats, except that it is charity for them.

Yes. Teaching and spreading beneficial knowledge is one of the three deeds that continue after death. The Prophet (ﷺ) said the best among people are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it. Sharing this page, teaching a skill, or passing on a useful lesson all fall under beneficial knowledge. Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 5027; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 1631.

Yes — nothing good is ever wasted. The Qur'an tells us that whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and that charity given in the way of Allah is multiplied like a single seed growing into seven ears, each with a hundred grains. Reference: Surah Az-Zalzalah (99:7); Surah Al-Baqarah (2:261).
