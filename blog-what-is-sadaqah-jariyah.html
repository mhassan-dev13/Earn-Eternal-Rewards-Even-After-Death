What is Sadaqah Jariyah?

Ongoing charity, explained simply: what it is, what counts, and how it differs from one-time sadaqah.

Sadaqah Jariyah is charity that keeps giving after the giver has gone. Unlike a one-time gift, whose benefit ends when it is used up, Sadaqah Jariyah keeps producing reward for as long as its benefit continues. That is why it is also called ongoing charity, continuous charity, and ceaseless charity.

The foundation of this idea is a well-known hadith. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: “When a person dies, his deeds come to an end except for three: ongoing charity (Sadaqah Jariyah), beneficial knowledge, or a righteous child who prays for him.” (Sahih Muslim 1631).

Three things in that hadith deserve attention. First, the reward is not tied to the size of the gift. A small gift that keeps helping can outlast a large gift that ends quickly. Second, the benefit must reach other people. Third, the reward continues even when the giver can no longer do anything new.

What counts as Sadaqah Jariyah?

The classic examples are easy to recognise: a masjid that people pray in, a water well that people drink from, a tree that gives shade and fruit, a Quran that people read from, and knowledge that people learn and pass on. In each case, the original act is finished, but its benefit goes on.

Other acts also count, and this is where many people miss opportunities. A traveller’s shelter, a school or hospital building, education for an orphan, and a waqf — an endowment whose benefit is dedicated for good — all fit the same pattern. The test is simple: does the benefit continue after you?

Sadaqah Jariyah, Sadaqah, and Zakat — the difference

  • Sadaqah (one-time): food for a hungry person today. The benefit is real, but it ends when the food is eaten.
  • Sadaqah Jariyah: a water well, a masjid, a tree, or knowledge that keeps benefiting people.
  • Zakat: the obligatory annual charity in Islam, with its own rules, rates, and eligible recipients.
  • Waqf: an endowment set aside so its benefit continues, often for generations.

How to start, even with little money

You do not need to build a whole masjid to start. A small contribution to a masjid project counts. Teaching one person a useful skill, sharing beneficial knowledge, or planting a tree are all within reach. The hadith about consistency is worth remembering here: the most beloved deeds to Allah are the most consistent, even if small (Sahih al-Bukhari 6465).

Start with one act, make it consistent, and let it grow. That is the practical meaning of Sadaqah Jariyah — a small seed that keeps giving, long after the hand that planted it is gone.
